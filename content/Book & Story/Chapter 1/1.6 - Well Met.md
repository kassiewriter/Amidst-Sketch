
Nuri: You… found an exit. That quickly? Please explain how.

Cal: Don’t act so impressed, I told you it wasn’t the end of my useless knowledge.

Nuri: You see, I can’t- Hey! Where are you going?

[Cal is already heading into the passageway that opened from his ambiguous puzzle-solving]

Nuri: You said you don’t know me, but at that pace it seems like you’d like to forget I was here, that *anyone* was here. I’m just lucky you weren’t here to get rid of me.

Cal: Slow down, I said I don’t know you, but it’d be a helpful start if I could actually see you clearly.

Nuri: Right, I forgot you can’t see well here…

Cal: I’ve felt you staring at me from a while ago, I’m not sure how, but you have some way of seeing me in this darkness. It wouldn’t be a fair conversation, no, interview- if we’re not on equal ground. Don’t you agree?

Nuri: Okayy, there’s no need to call this an interview, I practically invited myself. Anyone here should be in confusion, we all have motives to get out, so why not help your friends escape in the process if it makes the job easier? What I’ve been trying to tell you is that I can’t-

[Cal pulls out a lighter from his suit’s pocket and lights it up to a rough-scratchy texture on the wall]

Cal: There we go, a makeshift torch. Green man would be proud.

Nuri: Huh?

[As the room starts to light up with the fire on the wall growing, Nuri takes in the information and quickly tries to cover her face, albeit quite badly.]

Cal: That’s better. Ah- you’re a Nyo.

Nuri: Y-Yes.

Cal: I’ve heard a fair bit about your kind back at home. Agile, sly, thieves. Embarrassed when caught. They’re also reported to be able to see better at night, which is when most things tend to go missing. It explains how you’re able to see me, and why your eyes are glowing teal.

[Nuri regains her calm composure and stance before speaking]

Nuri: Well, you’ve got some of it right, but let me tell you just how wrong you are. First of all, we’re not thieves, and even if we were, it’s not as if you have anything of value on you.

Cal: Really? This is a one-off tie, you won’t find anything else like it in the world.

Nuri: Second, yes, we are ashamed of getting caught. But that’s for a reason. We can’t read common language, therefore, if it’s made public *what* we are… We become the targets of employers.
Things go well for the first few days after we think we’ve been found out. Weeks pass, maybe months. Life goes on as normal, ups and downs happen.
In one of those temporary down states, an opportunity arrives that could bring the quality of life much higher than any of those ups combined. This happens in a moment we’re off-guard, though, and there isn’t anyone around to translate the contract we’re proposed.
Just a coincidence, who cares? Better to take that opportunity than to let it pass and potentially never have the chance to see something like it again…

Cal: So, how bad can this contract be, knowing that you can’t read the terms and agreements of it?

Nuri: Some are wary, refusing to sign out of instinct. There’s also many who assume the best in others, and fall for their reassurance that the contract is perfectly safe to sign. It’s all a setup.

[Nuri takes a deep sigh but tries to smile before continuing]

Nuri: It is because of our differences that can be seen as advantageous that we’re overworked. It’s a little gruesome to really go into the details now, but this is also why we’re frightened when caught. A little reminder, I suppose, it’s only natural when we associate someone noticing with suddenly becoming the target of being silently watched - like hawks finding the moment to strike.

Cal: And why isn’t this stopped? Does anyone else know? Maybe we could go over there after we’re done, rescue the others.

Nuri: Hm, and since when did you grow a conscience?

Cal: No, it’s not that, I just find it a shame that they can’t read. It’s really such a wonderful thing, and hearing that there’s people refusing to teach others like you just to continue being able to abuse powers which aren’t theirs to begin with… It infuriates me.

Nuri: Thanks Cal. You know, maybe your suit *would* be worth stealing. Only if you wash it first.

[A short chuckle escapes Cal before they make their way into what awaits at the end of the tunnel.]
